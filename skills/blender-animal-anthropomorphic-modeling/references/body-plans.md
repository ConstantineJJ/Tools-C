# Animal body-plan notes

Use this as a decision aid, not as a substitute for species-specific reference.

## Mammalian quadruped limb landmarks

Forelimb chain:
`scapula -> humerus -> radius/ulna -> carpus (wrist) -> metacarpals -> digits`

Hindlimb chain:
`pelvis -> femur -> tibia/fibula -> tarsus/hock (ankle/heel region) -> metatarsals -> digits`

Common modeling error: interpreting the prominent rear hock as a backward-facing knee. The knee/stifle is higher and closer to the torso; the hock corresponds to the ankle/heel region.

### Foot posture classes

- **Plantigrade:** heel and sole broadly contact the ground (humans, bears).
- **Digitigrade:** weight is carried on the digits, with heel/hock elevated (cats, dogs).
- **Unguligrade:** weight is concentrated toward the tips of enlarged digits/hooves (horses, deer).

These categories guide joint placement and contact logic, but proportions vary strongly by species.

## Major masses before muscles

For a first blockout, read these as volumes:
- skull/muzzle;
- neck;
- ribcage/chest;
- pelvis;
- scapular/shoulder mass;
- upper/lower limb segments;
- paw/foot/hoof;
- tail base and taper.

The visible shoulder of a quadruped is strongly influenced by the scapula and muscles sliding over the ribcage. Do not attach the foreleg as a simple vertical cylinder at the bottom of the chest.

## Tail

Treat the tail as an extension of the vertebral chain:
- establish a believable root at the rear pelvis/spine region;
- preserve taper;
- allow sufficient segment count for required curvature;
- check collision/clearance against hind legs and body;
- avoid a cylindrical appendage that appears glued to the skin.

## Head and sensory features

Use species-specific reference for:
- skull slope and muzzle projection;
- eye position/orientation;
- ear root and cartilage shape;
- nose/nostril structure;
- jaw hinge and mouth corner;
- whisker pad or equivalent facial masses.

Stylization may change ratios, but keep the internal relationship coherent.

## Anthropomorphic blend map

For each region choose one of:
- `human-dominant`
- `animal-dominant`
- `hybrid`
- `stylized/non-anatomical by design`

Suggested regions:
`head/muzzle`, `neck`, `torso`, `shoulder/arm`, `hand/paw`, `pelvis`, `leg`, `foot`, `tail`, `ears`, `surface/fur`.

Do not assume one global percentage. A character can have a highly animal head, mostly human torso, digitigrade legs and grasping human-like hands.

## Non-mammalian safeguard

Birds, reptiles, amphibians, fish, arthropods and invented body plans require their own skeletal/functional references. Do not reuse mammalian joint chains or muscle flow simply because the silhouette is vaguely similar.
